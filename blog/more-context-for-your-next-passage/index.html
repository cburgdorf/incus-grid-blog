More context for your next passage: waves, lightning and 19 languages

Explore seasonal sea states, compare historical lightning with thunderstorm potential, and use IncusGrid in your language.

When we start thinking about the next sailing season, we rarely have just one question. What are the typical winds? How does the sea state change through the year? Does lightning activity increase later in the summer?

Since our introduction to the wind layers, we have added more ways to explore those questions. Some appeared briefly in our Android announcement; here is a closer look at the weather layers and the interface improvements that make them easier to use.

Updated October 2026: The waves layer now uses a higher-resolution wave model with five sea-state classes and more views, and the lightning layer now shows thunderstorm days instead of a choice of sources. The text and screenshots reflect the current app.

Waves: put the wind picture in context

Wind strength alone does not describe the sea you will meet. The Waves layer adds historical sea-state frequencies based on wave height and steepness. It is based on modelled wave data every three hours since October 2021.

Choose Calm (below 0.5 m), Slight (0.5–1.25 m), Moderate (1.25–2.5 m), Rough (2.5–4 m) or Very Rough (above 4 m), then select a month or the whole year. Short, steep seas count one class higher and long swell one class lower, because they feel that way on board. The colours show the percentage of modelled hours in the selected class. A higher value for Rough means that class occurred more often in the historical data; it is not a wave-height reading or a forecast for your passage.

Try the same area in January and July, keeping the class unchanged. Then compare Moderate with Rough for your intended month. This gives you another way to investigate seasonal differences before you start looking for a specific departure window.

Under More, the layer goes beyond the classes: the mean wave height, the share of short, steep sea, swell with arrows showing which way it runs, the swell period, and the number of calm days and rough days per month.

These are regional modelled patterns. A closer map view does not turn the layer into a harbour-scale prediction of swell or shelter.

Lightning: observations and favourable conditions

Under Lightning, the map now brings together three different views: Thunderstorm Days, Thunderstorm Potential and Terrain Exposure. Each answers a different question.

Thunderstorm Days shows how often lightning was actually recorded: the share of days in a month with lightning in the area. It is based on observations from a global ground-based lightning detection network and covers 2013–2025. Each value describes an area of about 0.5° (roughly 55 km), so the layer shows regional patterns rather than individual bays.

For a month-by-month look at the Adriatic, see our seasonal analysis of lightning patterns.

Lightning detection networks do not catch every strike, and their detection efficiency varies by region. Treat the values as a relative pattern: a lower recorded value is not evidence that lightning cannot occur there.

Thunderstorm Potential looks at the environment instead of recorded strikes. Based on 30 years of atmospheric data, it shows how often conditions favourable to thunderstorms occurred. It is a separate perspective: favourable conditions do not necessarily produce a storm, and the layer does not predict the next one.

Terrain Exposure brings the picture down to the scale of an anchorage. Zoom into a bay to explore how surrounding hills and headlands shape its modelled exposure to lightning. Even within the same bay, the terrain can make a difference, giving you another useful perspective when comparing potential places to anchor.

The map in your language

IncusGrid's app interface now supports 19 languages, including German, French, Spanish, Italian, Greek, Croatian, Dutch and Turkish.

The app uses your browser's language preferences where supported. You can also choose a language from the selector at the top, and the app remembers that choice. Controls, help text, month names and route details change with it; the underlying data stays the same.

This applies to the app interface. Geographic names and external services such as checkout may still use their own language settings.

Start with one area and one month

Pick a cruising area you know. Choose a month, explore the wind and wave layers in turn, then look at Thunderstorm Days and Thunderstorm Potential. Keeping the area and month consistent makes the differences easier to interpret.

IncusGrid helps you investigate historical patterns for planning. When it is time to sail, current forecasts, charts and local conditions still guide the passage itself.
