New on IncusGrid: Historical wind layers

IncusGrid now shows historical wind data as heatmaps. See how often any location experiences a specific wind condition — by speed, direction, and month.

We are excited to announce a major new feature: Wind Layers are now available on IncusGrid.

Just like our lightning and exposure layers, the wind layers are based on historical data and help you understand what to expect at any location worldwide. Let's walk through what's new.

Updated October 2026: The wind layers are now based on hourly weather model data at about 9 km resolution (2017–2025), and the controls have grown. All screenshots and numbers in this post come from the current version.

What the wind layers show

Each wind heatmap shows what share of hours a location experiences a particular wind condition. The color scale runs from pale yellow (rare) through orange and red to deep purple (frequent).

You can choose between two types of layers:

  • Speed categories: Calm (below 6 kn), Light (6–10 kn), Moderate (10–18 kn), Strong (18–30 kn) and Storm (above 30 kn)
  • Wind directions: N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW. They count the hours with at least 6 kn from that direction.

Each category has its own percentage scale that matches its typical frequency. Storm conditions top out at 20 %, because even the windiest places rarely see storm-force winds for long. Moderate wind uses a wider 0–80 % scale, and the direction layers go up to 60 %.

Example: The Meltemi in the Aegean

To see the layers in action, let's look at a well-known wind pattern: the Meltemi in the Aegean Sea.

The Meltemi is a strong northerly wind that blows across the Aegean during summer. Selecting the North direction layer for July makes this pattern immediately visible. Between Evia, Mykonos and Naxos, the wind comes from the north in about 60 % of all hours, the top of the scale.

Now switch to South wind for the same month.

The contrast speaks for itself. In July, southerly wind blows in at most about 3 % of hours across the Aegean.

The compass rose also shows that the Meltemi does not come from due north everywhere. In the North Aegean, it mostly blows from the northeast (about half of all hours). Around Santorini, Ikaria and Rhodes, a large share comes from the northwest (around a third of all hours near Santorini and Ikaria). Check the neighbouring directions for your own cruising area.

The seasonal shift

What happens when we move to November? The North wind layer is still active in the Aegean, but much less than in summer. In the Cyclades, the share drops from about 60 % to 30–40 % of hours.

And the South wind layer now shows activity where there was none in summer: around 10–15 % of hours in the Cyclades and more than 20 % in the North Aegean.

Being able to flip between months like this makes seasonal wind shifts easy to spot — not just in the Aegean, but anywhere in the world.

Storm detail layer

For storm-force winds, we offer two views: Storm (0–20 % scale) and Storm* (0–2 % scale).

The regular Storm layer is useful for identifying the world's stormiest regions. But in many sailing areas, storm percentages are low, often below 2 %. On the 0–20 % scale, these differences are hard to see.

That's where Storm* comes in. With its finer 0–2 % scale, subtle differences become visible. Two percent of a month is roughly 14 hours of storm-force wind — significant enough to factor into planning. In December, the North Aegean reaches this level, while most of the Cyclades stay well below 1 %.

Monthly breakdown

Like the lightning layers, all wind layers are available per month and as a yearly average. Use the timeline at the bottom of the screen to switch between months and watch patterns change through the seasons.

More since launch

Since this post was first published, the wind layers have learned a few new tricks. You can split every speed and direction layer by time of day, see the average wind speed, check how steady the wind is, and count storm days, gust days, quiet nights and the duration of strong wind. We explore the time-of-day layers in Two seas, two clocks.

Try it out

The wind layers are available now on IncusGrid. Select "Wind" in the layer toggle to get started. We'd love to hear your feedback.
