The next 10 days: IncusGrid's first forecast

IncusGrid now shows a 10-day forecast for wind, waves and thunderstorm risk on the same map as years of sailing data. Jump to a day with one tap or swipe through the hours. It is a small first step, and there is a lot more to come.

We started IncusGrid after lightning struck the water right next to our boat in the Ionian Sea. Afterwards we had questions that none of our sailing apps could answer. Would we have been safer in another bay? How often does this area get thunderstorms in October? Where do other boats actually go at this time of year, and where do they stay?

Weather apps are very good at telling you what is coming. What they leave out is what is normal, and what thousands of crews before you actually did. That is the gap we have been filling: thunderstorm risk down to single bays, wind and sea state for every month, real sailing routes and boat stays, and since this month weather windows on routes and shelter from wind at anchorages.

All of that looks back over many years. The obvious next question is what happens when you put it next to the weather that is actually coming. Today we take the first step.

Next 10 days: the forecast on the same map

The period selector at the bottom of the map has always had the months and the year. It now has a third choice: Next 10 days. Wind, Waves and Thunderstorm Risk switch from the season's normal to the forecast, and the months make way for the days and hours.

You move through the forecast in two ways:

  • Jump: tap a day, and the map shows it at the same time of day you were looking at.
  • Glide: swipe the hours, and the map follows hour by hour. It keeps going past midnight into the next day, so you can watch a front come through or the wind ease off overnight without breaking the flow. With a mouse, the scroll wheel moves one hour per notch, and the arrow keys work too.

What you see

  • Wind: speed or gusts in knots, with moving particles showing where the wind blows.
  • Waves: significant wave height, or the same sea-state classes as the monthly layer, with lines showing where the waves run.
  • Thunderstorm risk: none, low, moderate or high.

Every hour is shown for the first six days. After that the forecast is less certain, so it continues in 6-hour steps marked as a trend. The forecast comes from ECMWF's global model at about 9 km resolution, covers the whole world and is updated four times a day. The legend always shows when the forecast was made and when the next update is due.

The forecast, measured against the season

Tap the map and you get the forecast for that point, together with what IncusGrid already knows about it: how often it blows that hard or gets that rough at this place in this month. A 25-knot day means something different in the Meltemi than on a quiet stretch of coast. Now you can see which of the two it is, right next to the forecast.

This is the part we are most excited about, and it is only the beginning.

What comes next
