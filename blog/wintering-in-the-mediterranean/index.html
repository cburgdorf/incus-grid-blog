Wintering in the Mediterranean: where cruising boats actually spend the winter

Every autumn the same question: where do we leave the boat? Instead of asking around, we looked at real boat movements from recent years and counted where cruising boats stop for months, and when.

Every October the same question goes round the anchorages, the WhatsApp groups and the forums: where do we leave the boat for the winter? The answers are usually a mix of personal experience, a friend's recommendation and last year's prices.

We wanted to look at it differently. Not which place is said to be good, but where cruising boats actually stop for months.

How we counted

IncusGrid's Boat stays layer is built from thousands of real boat movements: the position reports of cruising boats. When a boat stops reporting its position for a while and later reports again close by, that pause is a stay. We grouped the stays into areas and counted, for each area, how many different boats paused there and for how long.

For this article we looked at the Mediterranean in recent years and kept only the long pauses: three to six months, and more than six months. They concentrate in a surprisingly small number of places.

A few honest caveats before the list:

  • A stay is a pause in position reports followed by a report nearby. It is a strong hint that a boat was left there, not a confirmed berth. Some boats were on the hard, some in the water, some on a mooring.
  • The data comes from boats that share their position, not from all yachts. The ranking says where more or fewer crews stayed, not how many boats a marina holds.
  • An area only shows up when several different boats paused there.

"Mostly" says which kind of long pause dominates. "Typical long stay" is the median length of the pauses longer than six months.

The regions

The Ionian. Preveza is the clear number one: no other area in the Mediterranean comes close. Many boats spend the winter on the hard in the big boatyards across the water from town, but wintering afloat in the marina or at anchor in the bay is not unusual either. Nearby, Mesolóngi on the mainland is in the top ten, and Nidri on Lefkada follows just behind, with most of its boats staying longer than six months. For crews who sail the Ionian in summer, the winter is literally around the corner.

Sicily and Malta. Marina di Ragusa and Licata on the south coast of Sicily are the two big wintering spots in the central Med, followed by the marinas around Msida in Malta. Both Sicilian harbours are known for a lively winter community of liveaboards. At Marina di Ragusa the data fits: about as many three to six month winter breaks as longer stays. At Licata most boats stay longer than six months.

Southeast Spain. Almerimar and Cartagena are the Spanish options in the top ten, with Sant Carles de la Ràpita in the Ebro delta further north. Cartagena stands out with a higher share of three to six month stays.

The Dodecanese. Leros appears twice: the boatyards in Partheni bay in the north and the harbour of Lakki in the south. Most pauses here are longer than six months, which fits an island where many crews haul out and fly home.

Peloponnese and around Athens. Kilada, Kalamata and Poros are smaller areas: Kilada and Kalamata follow right after the top ten, Poros a little further down. Two of them stand out for how long boats stay. Kilada is best known for its boatyards, Basimakopoulos in particular has an excellent reputation, and the typical long stay there is about eight and a half months. At Poros many boats spend the winter on a mooring buoy, and the typical long stay is about nine months, the longest of all areas in this article. Kalamata is more mixed, with as many winter breaks as boats left for longer.

Montenegro and Tunisia. Tivat and Herceg Novi in the Bay of Kotor come right after Nidri, with a balanced mix of winter breaks and longer stays. Tunisia shows two different patterns: at Monastir the two kinds of stay are about even, while at Hammamet most boats come for a classic winter break of three to six months, the opposite of Kilada and Poros.
