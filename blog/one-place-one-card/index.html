One place, one card: what's new in IncusGrid this October

Press and hold any place for wind, sea state, thunderstorms, boat stays and routes in one card. Plus weather windows on every route, shelter from wind at anchorages, bookmarks and a new phone layout.

Planning a stop used to mean switching between layers: wind, then waves, then thunderstorms, then the routes and where boats stay. This month we put all of that into one card, and added a few things that answer the next questions: when can I leave, and where do I anchor once I'm there.

We'll show them at one place many Atlantic crews pass in November: Mindelo on São Vicente, Cape Verde.

The spot report: everything about one place

Press and hold any place on the map (right-click with a mouse), and IncusGrid opens the spot report for the month you have selected:

  • Wind: the share of hours from calm to storm, and where it usually comes from
  • Sea state: from calm to very rough, and how often there is swell
  • Thunderstorms: thunderstorm days, the risk value and how much the terrain shields the spot
  • Boat stays and sailing routes within 5 nautical miles
  • Month by month: a traffic light for every month, so you see the season at a glance

Mindelo is a good example of why one card helps. The wind lights are red for most of the year: in November the trade wind blows at 18 knots or more about a quarter of the time. Yet the sea state stays green all year, in the lee of the islands around.

The lights are a quick guide for planning: green below 10 %, amber up to 25 %, red above 25 % of hours with strong wind, rough sea or days with thunderstorms. They are not a verdict on whether to sail.

Weather windows on every route

Knowing that a route is sailed in November is one thing. Knowing how often November actually gives you a good day to leave is another. The route card now has a Weather windows row: for every month, how many days were good for setting off in this direction.

For long passages only the first 24 hours are checked, because nobody can plan the middle of an ocean from a departure date.

For the run from the Canaries to Cape Verde the picture is clear. November and December are the months crews actually sail it, and they are also the months with the most good days: in November usually no wait at all, and only one time in ten three days or more.

Shelter from wind at anchorages

Back to Mindelo, with strong wind a quarter of the time: where do you drop the anchor? Under Boat stays, zoom in and tap an anchorage, marina or mooring field. The new Shelter from wind ring shows which directions the place is open to, and the petals inside show where the wind came from in the selected month. The darker part of each petal is the share of strong wind.

Mindelo Anchorage is open to the west and northwest. In November the wind blows into that opening 0 % of the time, also when it is strong. The same check works for any anchorage, from a Greek bay in the Meltemi to a Balearic cala before a mistral.

The ring comes from how much open water lies around the berth in each of 16 directions, and the wind from years of weather data.

Each area under Boat stays now also lists the anchorages and marinas where its boats actually lay, with their share for the selected month.

Smaller things

  • Bookmarks: save routes, spot reports and boat-stay areas, add a note ("ask about the fuel dock"), and find them again under Bookmarks on every device you sign in on. A free account keeps up to 500.
  • A new phone layout: more map, months and controls sized for fingers, cards that fold down to their title row, and the sea state and thunderstorm options in small docks above the month selector.
  • Android 0.5.0 is on Google Play with the spot report, Seabed and Thunderstorm Risk.
