It's here: IncusGrid for iPhone and iPad

The app you asked for most is in the App Store. Weather routing that keeps working offline, a protection score that tells you three days ahead whether you'll sleep or roll all night, sailing routes and their seasonality from years of recorded boat movements, and much more you haven't seen in any other sailing app.

When we launched the Android app in April, one question came back more than any other: and for iPhone? It has been the most requested feature ever since.

Today the answer is yes. IncusGrid is now in the App Store, for iPhone and iPad.

Download IncusGrid on the App Store →

The app arrives at a good moment. This autumn IncusGrid learned to plan passages on the forecast, to keep them working without a connection, and to answer the question every skipper asks at sunset: will this anchorage hold up tonight, and will we sleep? We'll show all three on one passage in the Windward Islands, where the season is just starting: from Rodney Bay on St Lucia to Admiralty Bay on Bequia, about 70 nautical miles south, next Tuesday.

When to leave: weather routing in your pocket

Set a start and a destination, and IncusGrid calculates a fast route for your boat on the 10-day ECMWF forecast: when you arrive, the strongest wind, gusts and waves on the way, and how many hours you sail upwind, reaching or under motor. We explained the planner in detail in From two clicks to a passage plan. On the iPhone it is the same planner, with the map taking most of the screen; on the iPad, the route and the result sit side by side.

Next week the trade wind is back: 13 to 14 knots from the east, a reach all the way south. Every departure sails without the engine and takes about twelve hours. So the question is no longer whether to go, but when.

That is what Compare departures is for. IncusGrid calculates the passage for every departure over the next two days and puts them side by side: arrival, duration, strongest wind, hours under motor, and the weather on the way. Here that last column decides it. Leave on Tuesday morning and you get an hour of rain. Leave late on Tuesday night or on Wednesday, and showers and thunderstorms move through the islands while you are out there.

Tuesday at 18:00 stays dry: an overnight reach, the anchor down in Admiralty Bay at 06:19 as the sun comes up.

Take the passage to sea, even without a connection

Between St Lucia and Bequia lie two countries, two SIM cards and a long stretch where the signal may simply be gone. That is exactly where you want your plan.

Before you leave, tap Make available offline on the saved passage. The app stores the passage together with its forecast and the coastline around it. At sea, without a connection, you can still:

  • follow the boat hour by hour against the plan,
  • move waypoints and recalculate the route from where you really are,
  • try a later departure while you are still at anchor.

The app always shows how old the stored forecast is. Once you are back online, it brings the passage up to the newest forecast by itself.

Where to spend the night: the protection score

Every cruiser knows the routine. You look at the wind forecast, then at the chart, then back at the forecast, and try to work out in your head whether the bay is open to where the wind is going. Then you guess about the swell. And at two in the morning you find out you guessed wrong, as the boat starts to roll.

IncusGrid now does that work for you. Tap any anchorage or mooring field, and you get its protection score for the next 72 hours: a number from 0 to 100 for every three hours, worked out for that exact bay from three things:

  • the forecast wind and gusts, and whether they blow into the bay or over the land around it,
  • the forecast waves and swell, including long-period swell that wraps around a headland into a bay that looks sheltered on the chart,
  • the shape of the bay itself: the directions it is open to, measured from the coastline all around.

Out of that comes a colour band across three days, so you see at a glance when a good anchorage turns bad: the evening the wind veers into the opening, the night the swell finds its way in. Every step says in plain words why it scores the way it does, like "Partly sheltered from the SSE wind, but gusts up to 16 kn".

And because the nights are what really matter, IncusGrid works out how your boat will lie. With the bow in the wind, does the swell come from ahead, at an angle or right on the beam? Each night gets its verdict, calm, slight rolling or rolly, and a small moon marks it on the band. When it gets rolly, the card suggests what helps: a stern anchor, a flopper stopper, or a line from the chain to the quarter to turn the bow into the swell. So you can pick the bay where you will actually sleep, not just the one where the anchor holds. Drag along the band and the map's forecast moves with you, with wind and swell drawn as arrows onto the bay's shelter ring.

Right below sits the seabed within a quarter of a mile: how much of the bottom around the anchorage is sand, seagrass, rock or reef, so you know where the anchor will hold before it goes down.

For Lower Bay the answer is easy right now: the bay opens to the west and north, the wind and waves come from the east and southeast, and the score stays at 100 for all 72 hours. The seabed tells you the rest: about a quarter of the bottom nearby is sand, the rest mostly reef and coral. Worth knowing before the anchor goes down.

Everything else is there too

The iPhone app is the full IncusGrid, with the same account as on the web and on Android. Your saved passages and bookmarks are on every device.

  • The spot report: press and hold any place for wind, sea state, thunderstorms and the seabed of a month on one card.
  • The next 10 days: wind, gusts and waves hour by hour on the map.
  • Sailing routes and boat stays: where other crews actually sail, and where they stay.
  • Thunderstorm risk down to single bays, and the seabed along the coast.

Sign in with Apple, Google or your email address, or just look around without an account. On the iPad, the bigger screen makes planning a season from the saloon table a pleasure.

Sailing with Android? Offline passages and anchorage protection are in the Android app as well.
